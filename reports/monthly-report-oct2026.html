North Oshawa New Home Market
October 2026

What the market did this month, where every competing site stands today, and what it means for Phase 2.

Prepared byJeff & Lorraine, Tanglewood
DateOctober 2026
DistributionBrian, Larry & Shimon
Data verifiedOctober 5, 2026

The biggest event in North Oshawa this month was Minto's launch of 20' freehold towns on October 3. By our count, 39 of 40 released lots sold in the first two days, priced from $596,000 to $703,000 after the HST rebate. Buyers are clearly in the market for freehold towns. The question for Phase 2 is the price point they are buying at. Every other builder held its price list and several are leaning harder on incentives. Dreamscape's and Heartwood Village's 30' collections have both sold out, with Heartwood's next 30' release coming soon, and Winchester by Menkes has sold out of several town models in its current release, which have been removed from its price list.

New home buyers are back, and they are buying towns and smaller singles. GTA single-family new home sales in August, the most recent month reported, ran 47% above their 10-year average, and the HST rebate is the main reason. North Oshawa is following the same pattern: the units moving fastest are the lowest price points in each community.

Investors are back at the table too. Under the 2026 Ontario Budget, homes bought as rental properties also qualify for the enhanced HST relief. Investors pay the HST at closing, then claim it back from the CRA once the home is leased to a tenant for at least 12 months.

Sources: BILD / Altus Group, GTA new home sales, August 2026 (released Sept 22)  |  2026 Ontario Budget: Enhanced HST Relief on New Homes  |  Osler: Ontario's new HST rebate, key details

Section B

Our Read on the Market

The market is finally starting to move. Buyers are back, and so are investors, who are taking advantage of the HST rebate and prices sitting at the bottom end of where they have been in years. What is moving is the smaller product. Minto's towns sold out in a weekend, and the 30' lots at both Dreamscape and Heartwood are gone.

The larger homes are a tougher sell, and in my opinion that comes down to who is buying them. Most of those buyers have a home to sell first. After the last few years, a lot of them are either nervous about selling into this market or don't have enough equity to make the move. Oshawa resale prices are back to roughly where they were in late 2021. In our own comp zone, the larger detached homes that sold this year took close to six months to sell and went for about 80% of their original asking price.
