Section A

Market Context and What Has Changed

Where the Market Stands Right Now

The North Oshawa new home market is operating in an environment shaped by genuine uncertainty. The global trade situation and its domestic ripple effects have created a level of caution that was not present six months ago. That said, the market is still transacting, and buyers who understand the current window are moving. A meaningful share of those buyers are motivated specifically by the current HST rebate structure, treating this as an opportunity to get into a new home before conditions shift.

The preconstruction market has found its floor. The trade situation creates two likely outcomes from here, and both point in the same direction. If material costs rise due to tariffs, construction cost inflation pushes new home prices up regardless of demand. If builders pull back and wait, supply tightens and the market tilts toward sellers. Either way, the buyers who act inside this window are the ones who capture today's pricing. The window does not stay open.

There is a further consideration for buyers shopping at Phase 2 price points. The current HST rebate structure meaningfully expands what buyers can afford today. Any change to the rebate threshold or structure reduces purchasing power for buyers in the $800K to $1.2M range -- exactly the Phase 2 buyer profile. The buyers who qualify and act now are buying at an effective price that may not be available to the next wave of purchasers.

For the builder, the same logic applies in reverse. Construction costs are still predictable. The window to sell Phase 2 at today's cost basis, before any further material or labour pressure works through, is open now. That window is unlikely to get wider.

The opportunity is real and the timing is now. Phase 2 has competitive pricing, a superior product, and a buyer pool that is alert to this moment. A well-positioned Phase 2 launch speaks directly to that buyer. This is the context for everything in this report.

The Fieldgate Price Move at Dreamscape

The most significant development in the comp set since August is Fieldgate raising prices across Dreamscape's 30' and 36' collections. Every 30' model increased $20,000 gross, which works out to $17,699 net for the buyer after the rebate. The 36' Astro and Mariner collections saw a $15,000 gross increase, approximately $13,274 net. The 42' Elevation A prices held flat, with minor adjustments on select Elevation B and C configurations only.

Fieldgate does not move prices at their highest-volume community in North Oshawa unless the sales pace supports it. The fact that they pushed is itself a demand signal, and it narrows the gap between Dreamscape's August prices and the Phase 2 suggested pricing we put forward. We cover that model by model in Section B.

Fieldgate also updated their incentive packages alongside the increases. 30' buyers now receive a $10,000 Decor credit plus central air and 5 appliances. The 36' and 42' packages carry a $15,000 Decor credit with the same inclusions. These inclusions soften the gross price increase for buyers, but the underlying direction of pricing is clear.

The Dreamscape Adjacency: Why Now Is the Right Time to Launch

Dreamscape is not simply a competitor in the North Oshawa market -- it is the community immediately adjacent to Tanglewood. That proximity creates a specific dynamic that is worth examining carefully before Phase 2 opens.

The instinct might be to wait for Dreamscape to sell out before launching Phase 2, removing a lower-priced competitor from the equation. The case against that approach is stronger. Right now, Tanglewood Phase 2 has a live comparison sitting next door. Buyers can visit both communities, walk both sites, and make a direct quality comparison. Phase 2 delivers broader lots, larger homes, more included features, and a superior finish package. That comparison works decisively in Phase 2's favour today.

When Dreamscape eventually closes, Phase 2 loses that comparison advantage -- and gains a different problem. The delivery of a large block of lower-priced, higher-density homes directly adjacent to an active Tanglewood sales site shapes how buyers and the broader market perceive the immediate area. More congestion, smaller homes, higher density: the neighbourhood character shifts at the moment those closings happen.

The window to sell Phase 2 as the clear premium choice in a live competitive market is open now. The opportunity to use Dreamscape's own product as the point of comparison -- to show buyers exactly what the price difference buys them -- is a sales story available today that will not be available once those homes are built and occupied. Phase 2 can win that comparison. It should be making that case now, while both products are in front of the same buyers.

Sales Activity and New Product in the Comp Set

At Winchester Estates, sales activity appears evident in the townhome collection based on changes to their available models on the pricelist. Heights of Harmony has announced plans to release freehold 2-storey townhomes this fall, up to 2,000 sq ft with private outdoor spaces -- adding a new product type to the Minto site that will compete directly in the freehold town segment. Minto also launched HOH Urban Towns, a stacked 2-bedroom townhome product priced from $409,933 net. All other competitors held their July and August price lists without change.

The HOH townhome announcement is worth noting specifically in the context of Phase 2's own town collection. Minto is signalling confidence in the North Oshawa freehold town market. Phase 2's 5-model town collection has an opportunity to get in front of that product before it launches.

Section D

Phase 2 Product Recommendation

If We Cannot Beat Them on Price, Beat Them on Quality

The comp set is holding prices and Fieldgate has demonstrated the market supports moving higher. Phase 2 does not need to be the cheapest option in North Oshawa. It needs to be the option where the buyer feels they got more. The way to achieve that is through a quality finishes package that is meaningfully better than the standard offering at any competing community, built into the base price so there are no surprises at the design centre and no awkward comparison when buyers research what they actually received.

◆
Quartz Countertops, All WashroomsQuartz in every washroom as standard. The comp set uses granite or laminate at the base. Quartz throughout is a visible, tactile differentiator buyers notice immediately.
◆
Stained Stairs + Matching Floor ColourStained oak staircase with a coordinated floor colour selection included. Competitors offer painted pine or a basic stain as an upgrade.
◆
Full Built-In Appliance PackageBuilt-in appliances including fridge and dishwasher to complete the kitchen. The comp set typically includes a basic freestanding package or omits fridge and dishwasher.
◆
Interlocking Brick DrivewayInterlocking driveway as standard. No comparable community in North Oshawa includes interlocking at the base. It photographs well and signals quality at the curb from day one.
◆
Pot Lights ThroughoutPot lights on the main floor included. Buyers expect them and routinely add them as upgrades elsewhere. Including them removes a cost shock at the design centre.
◆
Wrought Iron BalustersWrought iron balusters on the staircase as standard. A small visual detail that consistently elevates the perceived quality of the entry and main floor.
◆
Brushed Nickel Door HardwareBrushed nickel lever handles throughout. A simple, consistent finish choice that reads as premium relative to the standard builder brass or chrome lever.
◆
EV-Ready Electrical PortRoughed-in electrical port for an EV charger in the garage as standard. A forward-looking inclusion with high perceived value and low incremental construction cost.

These inclusions are not about competing with Dreamscape on square footage or lot width. They are about ensuring that a Phase 2 buyer who walks through the home feels they received something the market does not routinely offer. At the suggested price points, the buyer expects it. The cost of including these finishes is modest relative to the positioning benefit, and the alternative -- starting with a base product comparable to the comp set and charging upgrade premiums at the design centre -- gives buyers a reason to go elsewhere.

Ideas Worth Carrying Forward from the August Strategy Memo

In August we prepared a detailed strategy memo outlining eight product ideas for Phase 2. The September comp sweep reinforces the case for most of them. The comp set continues to offer relatively undifferentiated product, and several of these opportunities remain wide open in North Oshawa. The full memo with floor plan analysis is in Google Drive.

Idea 01 / All Lots / Highest Priority

Legal Secondary Suite: Included Basement Apartment

A fully legal, Oshawa-zoning-compliant basement apartment included as standard. No comp in North Oshawa is doing this. At current market rents, the financial case for buyers is immediate and significant.

Idea 02 / 36' and 40' / High Priority

Multi-Generational Suite on the Main Floor

A separate main-floor bedroom suite with private entrance for extended-family households. Durham Region has one of the highest concentrations of multi-generational households in the GTA and no builder in the North Oshawa comp set addresses it at the product design level.
