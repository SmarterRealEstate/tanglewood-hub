The North Oshawa competitive market has been largely stable since the previous update in July. Five of the six active builders held their price lists without change. The exception is Dreamscape by Fieldgate, which raised prices on both the 30' and 36' collections in August, a demand signal that narrows the gap between their range and Phase 2's suggested pricing. Against that backdrop, the case for opening Phase 2 isn't a matter of waiting for better conditions. The conditions are here. This report documents where each competitor stands today, presents the complete Phase 2 pricing with current market comparables alongside each lot width, and makes the argument for why now is the right moment for Medallion to bring Phase 2 to market.

Section A

Market Context and What Has Changed

Where the Market Stands Right Now

The North Oshawa new home market is operating in an environment shaped by genuine uncertainty. The global trade situation and its domestic ripple effects have created a level of caution that wasn't present six months ago. That said, the market is still transacting, and buyers who understand the current moment are moving. A meaningful share of those buyers are motivated specifically by the current HST rebate structure, treating this as an opportunity to get into a new home before conditions shift against them. The purchasers who qualify and commit now are buying at an effective price point that the next wave may not have access to.

The preconstruction market has found its floor. The trade environment creates two likely outcomes from here, and both point in the same direction. If material costs rise because of tariff pressure, construction cost inflation pushes new home prices higher regardless of demand. If builders pull back and wait, supply tightens and the market tilts toward sellers. Either way, the buyers who act within this window are the ones who capture today's pricing.

For Medallion, the same logic applies from the other direction. Construction costs are predictable today. Selling Phase 2 on a known cost basis is a material advantage over waiting through a period of rising input pressure. The opportunity to close that gap is available now.

The fundamentals are aligned. Phase 2 has competitive pricing, a superior product, and a buyer pool that's alert to this moment. A well-positioned launch speaks directly to that buyer. This is the context for everything in this report.

The Dreamscape Price Move

The most significant development in the comp set since the previous update is Fieldgate raising prices across Dreamscape's 30' and 36' collections. Every 30' model increased $20,000 gross, which works out to approximately $17,699 net after the rebate. The 36' collection saw a $15,000 gross increase, approximately $13,274 net. The 42' Elevation A prices held flat, with minor adjustments on select higher elevations only.

Fieldgate doesn't move prices unless the sales pace supports it. That decision is itself a data point. It also narrows the spread between Dreamscape's current pricing and the Phase 2 suggested range , which matters for the positioning story we lay out in Section B.

Fieldgate also updated their incentive packages alongside the increases. Buyers at the 30' width now receive a $10,000 decor credit plus central air and five appliances. The 36' and 42' packages carry a $15,000 decor credit with the same inclusions. These additions soften the effective sticker price for buyers, but the direction of pricing is clear.

Why Opening Phase 2 Now Is the Right Call

Dreamscape isn't simply another competitor in the North Oshawa market; it's the community situated directly adjacent to Tanglewood in the surrounding area. That proximity creates a specific set of dynamics worth examining carefully before any launch decision is made.

The instinct to wait for Dreamscape to sell out before opening Phase 2 is understandable. It's also the wrong call, in our view. Right now, with both communities in the market together, Tanglewood has something genuinely valuable: a live comparison. Buyers can visit both sites and see exactly what the price difference buys. Phase 2 delivers larger homes and a finished recreation room as part of the standard package. Dreamscape does offer decor credits and appliances as purchase incentives, which softens part of that picture, but incentives are temporary; a better-specified home is a permanent decision for the buyer. We haven't done a full side-by-side of both feature sheets yet, but the opportunity to position Phase 2 as the premium, thoughtfully finished product in this pairing is real. Realizing that opportunity likely means adding or upgrading select features and finishes before launch.

There's also a longer-term argument for opening Phase 2 now. If Tanglewood waits until Dreamscape finishes selling before launching, the surrounding area fills in with a large block of smaller, more compact homes before a single Phase 2 home is sold. Opening Phase 2 while Dreamscape is still actively selling changes that picture. The two communities build out together, the area develops a real mix of home sizes and price points, and Phase 2 isn't launching into a neighbourhood already shaped entirely by a more compact, standardized product. Selling into a balanced neighbourhood is a better position than selling into one that isn't. It protects Phase 2's pricing power and puts Tanglewood in a stronger spot for future releases when Fieldgate has long since moved on.

Sales Activity and New Product in the Comp Set

At Winchester Estates, sales activity is evident in the townhome collection based on changes to their available model offerings on the current price list. Heights of Harmony has announced plans to release freehold 2-storey townhomes this fall, adding a product type to their site that will compete directly in the freehold town segment. Heartwood Village, Homeward Hills, The Views by Jeffery Homes, and Heights of Harmony all held their price lists without change since the previous competitive update in July.

The Heights of Harmony townhome announcement has a specific implication for Phase 2. Minto is making a public commitment to the freehold town market in North Oshawa. Phase 2's townhome collection has a clear opportunity to be in market before that product arrives. The window to be first in that segment is short.
