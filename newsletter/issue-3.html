Real TalkTrade Watch: October

A new round of tariffs, and new home buyers are showing up anyway. The headlines have been loud. What matters is what is actually happening on the ground in North Oshawa, and what it means for the decision you may be thinking about.

By Jeffrey Villeneuve, Broker
This Month in Three Numbers
692
GTA single-family new home sales, August
47% above the 10-year average. The HST rebate is doing its job.
25–50%
Counter-tariffs, in effect Sept 8
On U.S. lumber, plywood, steel and aluminum. Cost pressure on every home still being built.
2.25%
Bank of Canada rate, held Sept 2
Trade named as the main risk. Next decision: October 28.

Sources: BILD / Altus Group (Sept 22), Department of Finance Canada, Bank of Canada.

Here is the good news first. New home buyers are back. Across the GTA, single-family new home sales in August ran well above their 10-year average, and the HST rebate is the main reason. Buyers who sat on the fence for two years are signing again.

Now the real talk. On September 8, Canada answered the latest U.S. tariffs with counter-tariffs of its own, and a lot of them land on building materials: lumber, plywood, steel and aluminum. Builders pay more for every home still under construction. That does not show up in prices overnight, but it points one way. Today's new home prices may be the low point.

Resale is another story. In Oshawa, the average August sale was $652,775 (TRREB Market Watch, August 2026), and sellers are competing with each other for a smaller pool of buyers. You can see it in our own comp zone: eight homes listed and none sold since July.

For buyers: this is a good window, on both new and resale. For sellers: price matters more than it has in years. The homes that sold this year were priced right from day one.

Homeowner Corner

Your Winter-Ready Checklist

Ten minutes this month can save you a service call in January. Here is what I'd tick off before the first freeze.

Shut off outdoor tapsClose the inside shut-off valve, then open the outside tap to drain it.
Change the furnace filterA clean filter helps the furnace and cuts dust once the windows stay shut.
Check your gradingSoil should slope away from the foundation. Newer lots settle in the first few years.
Clean the eavestroughsLeaves plus freezing rain can mean ice dams. Make sure the downspouts drain away from the house.
Test smoke and CO alarmsHeating season is CO season. Test them now and swap any old batteries.
Newer owners: check your warranty datesIf you moved in within the last couple of years, look over your paperwork before year end.
